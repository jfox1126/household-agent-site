Privacy policy

household-agent · last updated 9 October 2026

household-agent is a private program used by the two adults of a single household. It is not offered to the public and has no other users. This policy describes what it reads, what it may create, where information goes, and how to stop it.

What it accesses

With the account holder's consent, household-agent may hold two Google Calendar permissions: calendar.events.readonly, which lets it read events, and calendar.events, which also lets it create, change and delete them. For its owner's own Gmail accounts, and no one else's, it may also hold gmail.readonly, which lets it read email but not send, change or delete any. On its owner's services account only, it may also hold gmail.send, which lets it send email but not read, change or delete any. On that account it holds both together, so it can send email and read the whole inbox there, but it reads only the replies in threads it started. It requests no other Google permission.

It creates an event only on the one shared calendar it is set up to use, and only when one of the two adults asks for it: by typing or pasting the event to it, which adds it at once with a way to undo it, or by saying yes to an event it read from a picture they sent, or by pressing a button to confirm it. It never creates an event on its own. It deletes an event only if it created that event and a person undoes it. It never changes or deletes any other event, and it never adds guests, so it never sends an invitation.

From each event it reads, it uses the title, the start and end times, whether the event lasts all day, whether it is marked busy or free, whether invitees have replied, whether it is tentative or private, and whether the calendar's owner organized it. It does not use event descriptions, locations or conference links.

It reads none of its owner's email except the threads it started, described next. No other email, sender, subject or text is read, stored, shown in a chat, or sent to anyone.

It sends email to one address only: a personal assistant service its owner uses and chose. When an adult it is set up to accept asks it something it cannot do itself, it emails that service the one line that was typed, with the first names of the adults who will see the answer. Each question gets its own email thread. It shows the service's reply in the chat where the question was asked, and does not keep the reply. It sends nothing else from the household's calendars, chats or email.

Since 9 October 2026, its owner may also set it to keep a standing watch with that service: it emails the service a written brief of what to look for in its owner's school email, once, and again only if the brief changes or a send did not go through. The service reads that email under its own arrangement with the owner; household-agent does not. It then reads only that one thread and uses only the service's replies in it. From them it keeps reference numbers, for about a week, never their text. Nothing from a watch is shown in a chat yet. Before it uses email for anything more, this page will say what, and the change will be dated.

It reads the household's group chats and each adult's own chat with its account, and acts on no other chat, except to tell anyone else who sends it /start their own Telegram ids. On its owner's computer it also reads, from his own Messages, which people he has written to are waiting on his reply and since when, named from his Contacts, never what was said, and tells him alone, once a day.

Where the information goes

  • The program runs on one personal computer, and its database is on that computer.
  • Summaries and replies are sent through Telegram, or through Apple's iMessage, to chats readable only by the two adults in the household and the program's own account: the household's group, and each adult's own chat with it, for what is meant for that adult alone. Since 9 October 2026 that includes each adult's own iMessage chat with it, where for now it answers only a request to stop.
  • Each line either adult types in the household's group chats or in their own Telegram chat with it, and each picture sent there, goes to Anthropic, which provides the language model that decides whether it is meant for the program and what it asks. The household's open to-do list goes with it. Calendar events and email are not sent to Anthropic.
  • An event it creates is stored by Google on the calendar, like any other event.
  • Questions it cannot answer itself are emailed to the one personal assistant service its owner chose, and that service's replies are shown in the chat.
  • It also tells a small watcher, run from its owner's Google account, that it is running; the watcher emails him if it stops. Nothing else is in that signal.
  • Nothing is sold, published or used for advertising, and nothing is shared with anyone else.

How long it is kept

Calendar events are read when they are needed and are not stored; the program keeps a record of what it has sent so it does not repeat itself. Email is read when a reply is shown, or when a watch's reply is checked, and its text is not stored; a watch keeps its replies' reference numbers for about a week. Text a person sends it may be kept on the computer, with a record of what was done with it, until its owner deletes it. Each chat line, as it arrives, is also kept on the computer for two days. Deleting the program's database, and the copies of it kept from its last five updates, removes everything it has recorded. Events it has created stay on the calendar, like any other event, until someone deletes them.

Credentials

Its credentials are stored in files on the computer that only the program and its owner can read. Each is sent only to the service that issued it, and none is displayed or logged.

Withdrawing access

The account holder can revoke household-agent's access at any time, with immediate effect, from the Google Account permissions page at myaccount.google.com/permissions. No request to us is needed. Once revoked, the program can no longer read or change the calendar, or read or send email.

household-agent's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Either adult can stop it sending anything by sending /stop in any of its chats.
